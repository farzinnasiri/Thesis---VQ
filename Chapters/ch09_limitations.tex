\chapter{Limitations and Scope}
\label{ch:limitations}

This thesis evaluates tokenizer behavior, not a complete autoregressive image
generation system. The experiments make several comparisons possible, but they
also set clear limits on what can be concluded.

\section{Comparative Scope}
\label{sec:limitations_comparative_scope}

The study compares one VQGAN tokenizer and one LlamaGen VQ-16 tokenizer. They
share the same external interface, a $16\times16$ grid and a nominal
16{,}384-code vocabulary for $256\times256$ images, but they differ in
architecture, training procedure, loss functions, codebook dimensionality, and
normalization. The observed differences therefore describe the evaluated models
as complete systems. They do not isolate the effect of any one architectural
choice, nor do they establish that the results generalize to all VQGAN-style or
LlamaGen-style tokenizers.

The codebook analysis also distinguishes nominal and empirical vocabulary size.
VQGAN's sparse active subset is an observed property of this checkpoint on the
tested data. It should not be read as a statement about every possible input
distribution or every VQGAN checkpoint. Conversely, LlamaGen's broad code usage
does not by itself establish that its individual code IDs are more semantic or
more stable.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:limitations_protocol}

All reconstructions use a fixed $256\times256$ preprocessing pipeline. This
provides a common interface but can change the statistics of datasets with
different native resolutions, aspect ratios, or visual structure. In
particular, document pages and medical images are not naturally distributed like
ImageNet photographs. Their results should be interpreted as the response to
the processed inputs used here, rather than as a general assessment of either
tokenizer on the underlying application domain.

The perturbation experiments use selected noise levels, patch sizes, and token
replacement rules. Global noise measures stability under one image-space
corruption family. Local image-space and token-space patches are placed at
seeded, uniformly sampled valid locations. The two model implementations use
the same seeds and intervention levels, but the random noise arrays and patch
locations were not exported as a shared cross-model intervention manifest.
These interventions are useful controlled probes, but they do not span the
full set of natural corruptions, adversarial changes, or semantic image edits
that may occur in practice.

The study also reports aggregate quantities. Token flips are averaged over all
positions and images, while codebook statistics pool assignments over each
dataset. These summaries reveal distributional changes but do not identify what
visual concept each individual code represents. The analysis does not train a
downstream autoregressive model, so its claims concern the token distributions
and token stability presented to such a model rather than the final generation
quality of a full system.

Absolute token-flip rates should also not be read as a calibrated robustness
ranking across the two tokenizers. Their codebooks induce different latent
partitions and have very different empirical vocabulary sizes. The rates are
most directly interpretable as within-tokenizer changes over perturbation
strength.

\section{Metrics and Spatial Analysis}
\label{sec:limitations_metrics}

PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FID measure different properties of reconstructions and
do not provide a complete account of perceptual or semantic quality. They can
also rank datasets differently because a structured medical image or document
page can be easy to reconstruct while still being far from ImageNet in token
distribution. For this reason, reconstruction quality and distribution shift
are presented as complementary rather than interchangeable measurements.

The global distribution-shift results are the strongest evidence in
Chapter~\ref{ch:rq5}. The per-position entropy maps and code-specific spatial
maps are more tentative. Positional entropy is sensitive to sample size: for
example, ImageNet-V2 has 10{,}000 images, so its entropy is bounded by fewer
observations than the 50{,}000-image ImageNet reference. Code-specific maps are
especially sparse for LlamaGen because its assignments are distributed over
16{,}384 codes. The maps suggest that shift changes where some codes appear,
but they do not yet support a code-level semantic interpretation.

Some spatial patterns can also reflect preprocessing rather than learned
semantics. Resizing and center cropping interact with aspect ratio, and the
boundary pattern observed for ObjectNet is consistent with
preprocessing-induced borders. The spatial maps therefore describe the
tokenizers' response to the evaluated tensors, not an invariant property of
the source datasets.

\section{Implications for Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations_future_work}

The next step is to connect tokenizer-level probes to downstream generation.
Training or evaluating autoregressive priors on the measured token streams would
test whether high flip rates, concentrated usage, or distribution-dependent
spatial allocation predict generation quality and robustness. Controlled
ablations that vary one tokenizer design choice at a time would also separate
codebook size, training objective, and architecture. Finally, larger and
matched-size domain datasets would make the spatial analyses less dependent on
finite-sample effects.

\section{Research Questions and Thesis Overview}
\label{sec:research_questions}

This thesis studies discrete image tokenizers used in autoregressive visual generation. The central premise is that reconstruction quality alone is not enough to characterize a tokenizer. A tokenizer also defines a visual vocabulary, a token distribution, and a spatial response to perturbations. These properties determine what the downstream autoregressive model sees and how local or global its errors may become.

The experiments are organized around five research questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item How do the tokenizers compare in reconstruction quality and empirical codebook usage?
\item How stable are their reconstructions and token assignments under global perturbations?
\item How local is the encoder response when a perturbation is applied to a local image patch?
\item How local is the decoder response when a local patch of visual tokens is edited directly?
\item How do reconstruction quality and codebook usage change under distribution shift?
\end{enumerate}

These questions decompose the tokenizer into the functional boundaries relevant to autoregressive image generation: reconstruction behavior, codebook behavior, encoder-side stability, decoder-side locality, and robustness under distribution shift.

The remainder of the thesis follows this decomposition. Chapter~\ref{ch:background} gives the conceptual background on autoregressive image modeling and vector-quantized tokenizers. Chapter~\ref{ch:datasets_models_metrics} defines the models, datasets, and metrics. The subsequent experimental chapters evaluate reconstruction and codebook usage, global perturbation robustness, local image-space perturbations, token-space perturbations, and distribution shift, before the final discussion synthesizes the results across these probes.

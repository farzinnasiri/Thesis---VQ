\chapter{Reconstruction Baseline and Codebook Usage}
\label{ch:rq1}

Before any perturbation is introduced, this chapter establishes what the two tokenizers do under ordinary conditions: reconstructing ImageNet validation images through the full encode--quantize--decode pipeline. Two questions are examined jointly. How well do VQGAN and LlamaGen reconstruct images under a shared protocol? And how do they use their nominally equal codebooks in doing so? Reconstruction metrics capture the quality of $\hat{x}$ relative to $x$; codebook statistics capture the empirical token distribution the image passes through before decoding. The two dimensions are related but not redundant: a tokenizer can score well on reconstruction while using only a narrow slice of its vocabulary, or spread assignments broadly while still introducing reconstruction error.

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:rq1_motivation}

The later chapters perturb the tokenizer in several ways: global image noise, local image-space perturbations, token-space edits, and dataset shifts. Those analyses are only interpretable if the unperturbed baseline is already understood. A robustness curve, for example, is meaningful only relative to the clean reconstruction quality of the model. Similarly, a token replacement experiment depends on which tokens are actually used by the tokenizer on the reference distribution.

The baseline is also important because both tokenizers have the same nominal vocabulary size, $K = 16{,}384$, and the same spatial token grid, $16 \times 16$, for $256 \times 256$ images. At the level of external interface, both models produce 256 discrete image tokens per image. This makes them directly comparable as visual tokenizers. However, the same interface can hide very different internal behavior. A full codebook and a sparse codebook both expose a vocabulary of 16,384 possible indices, but the sequence model trained on top of the tokenizer sees only the empirical distribution of those indices.

Reconstruction quality measures whether the autoencoder path preserves images well. Codebook usage characterizes what visual alphabet the tokenizer actually presents to the downstream model. The two are not redundant: a high-fidelity reconstruction tells nothing about whether the underlying token sequence is diverse or degenerate.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:rq1_experimental_setup}

The experiment uses the ImageNet validation set described in Chapter~\ref{ch:datasets_models_metrics}. Each image is resized and center-cropped to $256 \times 256$, converted to RGB, and passed through the tokenizer-specific preprocessing pipeline. Both tokenizers are evaluated in deterministic inference mode. No augmentation is applied at evaluation time.

For each image $x$, the tokenizer produces a reconstruction:

\begin{equation}
\hat{x} = D(Q(E(x))),
\end{equation}

where $E$ is the encoder, $Q$ is the vector-quantization step, and $D$ is the decoder. Reconstruction quality is measured using the metrics defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:datasets_models_metrics}: PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID, sFID, precision, recall, and Inception Score. Codebook usage is computed from the discrete token grids over all 50,000 validation images. Since each image produces a $16 \times 16$ grid, each tokenizer contributes:

\begin{equation}
50{,}000 \times 16 \times 16 = 12{,}800{,}000
\end{equation}

token assignments for the ImageNet baseline.

The codebook analysis reports both raw and effective vocabulary use. The raw active count is the number of codebook entries assigned at least once. The effective active count uses the minimum-frequency criterion from Chapter~\ref{ch:datasets_models_metrics}, with a threshold of $10^{-6}$ of all token assignments. Entropy and perplexity summarize the effective size of the empirical distribution, and top-$k$ mass measures how much of the token stream is captured by the most frequent codes.

\section{Codebook Usage}
\label{sec:rq1_codebook_usage}

LlamaGen reconstructs ImageNet images more accurately under the chosen metrics. The codebook analysis examines whether this advantage is accompanied by a different use of the discrete vocabulary. Table~\ref{tab:rq1_codebook_usage} reports the main usage statistics.

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \small
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
    \begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
        \textbf{Tokenizer} & \textbf{Active} & \textbf{Effective active} & \textbf{Perplexity} & \textbf{Top-10} & \textbf{Top-100} & \textbf{Top-500} \\ \hline
        LlamaGen & 16{,}384 & 16{,}384 & 16{,}179.19 & 0.003 & 0.012 & 0.046 \\
        VQGAN    & 972      & 971      & 910.46    & 0.022 & 0.165 & 0.665 \\
    \end{tabular}
    \caption{ImageNet codebook usage summary. Active counts report how many of the 16,384 nominal codes appear on ImageNet. Effective active counts use the minimum-frequency threshold described in Chapter~\ref{ch:datasets_models_metrics}. Top-$k$ values report the fraction of all token assignments covered by the most frequent $k$ codes.}
    \label{tab:rq1_codebook_usage}
\end{table}
\FloatBarrier

The difference is large. LlamaGen uses all 16,384 codebook entries on ImageNet with a perplexity of 16,179, indicating that assignments are spread nearly uniformly across the full vocabulary. VQGAN uses only 972 of 16,384 codes (about 5.9\% of the nominal codebook), and its perplexity of 910 is close to that raw active count, meaning the active subset is both small and densely used.

LlamaGen's top-500 codes account for only 4.6\% of all token assignments; VQGAN's top-500 account for 66.5\%. Despite identical nominal vocabulary sizes, the token stream VQGAN produces is far more concentrated.

Figure~\ref{fig:rq1_code_usage_sorted} shows the full rank-frequency curve. The LlamaGen curve is much flatter; the VQGAN curve drops steeply and terminates because most of the nominal vocabulary goes unused on ImageNet. The two models differ not only in reconstruction quality but in the empirical vocabulary they present to an autoregressive prior.

\section{Joint Reconstruction--Codebook View}
\label{sec:rq1_joint_view}

Table~\ref{tab:rq1_joint_summary} places the reconstruction and codebook results side by side. LlamaGen reconstructs better and uses the full codebook densely. VQGAN reconstructs less accurately under the chosen metrics and uses a much smaller active subset of the codebook.

\FloatBarrier

Despite identical sequence length and nominal vocabulary size, the two tokenizers produce very different empirical token distributions. Any token-space intervention must account for this asymmetry: drawing replacements from the full nominal codebook is appropriate for LlamaGen, whose empirical vocabulary covers it, but would inject dead codes for VQGAN.

The result also rules out one simple explanation of the quality gap. LlamaGen does not reconstruct better because it concentrates on a few high-quality codes; it reconstructs better while distributing assignments across nearly the entire codebook. VQGAN's lower scores accompany a sparse empirical vocabulary. The baseline cannot attribute this to any single design choice (the two models differ along multiple dimensions), but establishes that reconstruction quality and codebook density pattern together here.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:rq1_discussion}

In discrete representation learning, codebook collapse---many entries going unused---is a well-documented failure mode, and one that LlamaGen demonstrably avoids on a 16,384-entry codebook. VQGAN shows partial collapse: roughly 94\% of nominal codes are inactive on ImageNet, and the active subset carries nearly all the reconstruction load.

The practical consequence for subsequent chapters is asymmetric. Token replacements drawn from the full nominal codebook are appropriate for LlamaGen, whose empirical vocabulary covers it; for VQGAN, the same procedure would inject codes the decoder rarely encounters at inference time. The token-space experiments in later chapters account for this by restricting VQGAN replacements to the active subset.

The baseline is ImageNet-specific: VQGAN may activate a different code subset on other domains, so the sparse pattern observed here does not imply global codebook collapse. The reconstruction advantage cannot be attributed to any single design choice either---the two models differ in architecture, training objective, and codebook initialization---and the correlation between codebook density and reconstruction quality observed here motivates, but does not resolve, the question of causation.

\chapter{Reconstruction Baseline and Codebook Usage}
\label{ch:rq1}

Before any perturbation is introduced, this chapter establishes the clean ImageNet baseline for the two tokenizers. The purpose is to define the reference point used by later chapters: how well each tokenizer reconstructs unmodified validation images, and what empirical token distribution it produces while doing so. Reconstruction metrics describe the quality of $\hat{x}$ relative to $x$; codebook statistics describe the discrete vocabulary actually used by the tokenizer before decoding.

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:rq1_motivation}

The later chapters perturb the tokenizer in several ways: global image noise, local image-space perturbations, token-space edits, and dataset shifts. Those analyses are only interpretable if the unperturbed baseline is already understood. A robustness curve, for example, is meaningful only relative to the clean reconstruction quality of the model. Codebook measurements also need a clean reference distribution: without it, later changes in token usage cannot be separated from the tokenizer's ordinary behavior on ImageNet.

The baseline is also important because both tokenizers have the same nominal vocabulary size, $K = 16{,}384$, and the same spatial token grid, $16 \times 16$, for $256 \times 256$ images. At the interface level, both models produce 256 discrete image tokens per image. This makes them directly comparable as visual tokenizers, while still leaving open whether their architectures induce the same empirical vocabulary.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:rq1_experimental_setup}

The experiment uses the ImageNet validation set described in Chapter~\ref{ch:datasets_models_metrics}. This is an in-domain baseline: both tokenizers are evaluated on the same ImageNet validation distribution used in their original ImageNet-oriented evaluation, not on a shifted domain. Each image is resized and center-cropped to $256 \times 256$, converted to RGB, and prepared using tokenizer-specific preprocessing pipelines. Both tokenizers are evaluated in deterministic inference mode. No augmentation is applied at evaluation time.

For each image $x$, the tokenizer produces a reconstruction:

\begin{equation}
\hat{x} = D(Q(E(x))),
\end{equation}

where $E$ is the encoder, $Q$ is the vector-quantization step, and $D$ is the decoder. Reconstruction quality is measured using the metrics defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:datasets_models_metrics}: PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID, sFID, precision, recall, and Inception Score. Codebook usage is computed from the discrete token grids over all 50,000 validation images. Since each image produces a $16 \times 16$ grid, each tokenizer contributes:

\begin{equation}
50{,}000 \times 16 \times 16 = 12{,}800{,}000
\end{equation}

token assignments for the ImageNet baseline.

The codebook analysis reports both raw and effective vocabulary use. The raw active count is the number of codebook entries assigned at least once. The effective active count uses a minimum-frequency threshold of $10^{-6}$ of all token assignments, corresponding to at least 13 assignments out of 12.8 million tokens in this experiment. Entropy and perplexity summarize the effective size of the empirical distribution, and top-$k$ mass measures how much of the token stream is captured by the most frequent codes.

\section{Reconstruction Baseline}
\label{sec:rq1_reconstruction_baseline}

Table~\ref{tab:rq1_reconstruction_metrics} reports the ImageNet reconstruction metrics for LlamaGen and VQGAN. LlamaGen improves the paired-image metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and the reference-conditioned distribution metrics (FID, sFID, precision, and recall). Inception Score is retained as a diagnostic of classifier confidence and class diversity, not treated as a paired reconstruction metric or as having a universally preferred direction. The remaining metrics make LlamaGen the stronger clean reconstruction baseline on ImageNet.

\end{table}

The PSNR gap is modest in absolute terms, but it is consistent with the other metrics. LlamaGen reaches a mean PSNR of 20.79 compared with 19.65 for VQGAN, and a mean SSIM of 0.558 compared with 0.489. LPIPS also favors LlamaGen: 0.228 compared with 0.286 for VQGAN. Since LPIPS is computed in a learned feature space, this indicates that the reconstruction difference is not only a pixel-level effect. The distributional metrics agree with the same direction: LlamaGen obtains FID 2.19 and sFID 5.01, while VQGAN obtains FID 5.06 and sFID 6.49.

The corresponding per-image standard deviations are 3.18 dB, 0.167, and
0.058 for LlamaGen PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, and 3.41 dB, 0.176, and 0.066 for
VQGAN. The distributions therefore overlap substantially even though all
three dataset means favor LlamaGen. FID and related distribution metrics do
not decompose into per-image observations and are consequently reported as
dataset-level estimates.

\section{Codebook Usage}
\label{sec:rq1_codebook_usage}

The metrics above show that LlamaGen reconstructs ImageNet validation images more accurately than VQGAN under the shared protocol. The following analysis examines whether the two tokenizers also differ in how they use their discrete vocabularies. Table~\ref{tab:rq1_codebook_usage} reports the main usage statistics.

\begin{table}[t]
    \centering
    \small
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
    \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{%
    \begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrr}
        \textbf{Tokenizer} & \textbf{Codebook size} & \textbf{Active} & \textbf{Effective active} & \textbf{Perplexity} & \textbf{Top-10} & \textbf{Top-100} & \textbf{Top-500} \\ \hline
        LlamaGen & 16{,}384 & 16{,}384 (100.0\%) & 16{,}384 (100.0\%) & 16{,}179.19 & 0.003 & 0.012 & 0.046 \\
        VQGAN    & 16{,}384 & 972 (5.9\%)       & 971 (5.9\%)       & 910.46    & 0.022 & 0.165 & 0.665 \\
    \end{tabular}
    }
    \caption{ImageNet codebook usage summary. Active counts report how many nominal codes appear on ImageNet; effective active counts use the threshold of at least 13 assignments. Top-$k$ values report the fraction of all token assignments covered by the most frequent $k$ codes.}
    \label{tab:rq1_codebook_usage}
\end{table}

LlamaGen uses all 16,384 codebook entries on ImageNet with a perplexity of 16,179. This means that the empirical token distribution is close to uniform over the full nominal vocabulary. VQGAN uses only 972 of 16,384 codes, or about 5.9\% of the nominal codebook. This drop is caused by dead or inactive codes: most nominal entries are never assigned to any ImageNet validation patch. Its perplexity of 910 is close to the raw active count, so the active subset is small but not dominated by only a handful of codes.

LlamaGen's top-500 codes account for only 4.6\% of all token assignments; VQGAN's top-500 account for 66.5\%. Thus, although the two tokenizers expose the same nominal vocabulary size, the sequences they would provide to a downstream autoregressive model have very different empirical vocabularies. The downstream model is trained on this empirical token stream, not on the unused portion of the nominal codebook.

Entropy gives an idealized coding interpretation of the same result. Since
$H=\log(\operatorname{PPL})$, the observed perplexities correspond to about
$9.69$ nats ($13.98$ bits) per token for LlamaGen and $6.81$ nats ($9.83$
bits) per token for VQGAN. For a 256-token grid, these are approximately
$3{,}579$ and $2{,}516$ idealized bits per image under an optimal code matched
to each empirical marginal distribution. LlamaGen's empirical marginal
entropy is therefore about 42\% higher. This is a coding-rate statement, not a
claim that individual LlamaGen tokens contain 42\% more semantic information.

Figure~\ref{fig:rq1_code_usage_sorted} shows the full rank-frequency curve. LlamaGen assigns probability mass across the full codebook. VQGAN has a shorter curve because most nominal codes are inactive on ImageNet, and its active codes carry substantially higher average frequency.

\begin{figure}[t]

    \caption{Sorted ImageNet code-usage distribution. Codes are ranked by empirical frequency. The VQGAN curve ends near rank 972 because the remaining nominal codes are inactive on ImageNet.}
    \label{fig:rq1_code_usage_sorted}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:rq1_code_usage_concentration} gives a complementary view, reporting the cumulative mass of the top-10, top-100, and top-500 codes. High top-$k$ mass means that a large fraction of the training sequence is made from a small set of token IDs. For an autoregressive model trained on these sequences, this changes the effective vocabulary and token-frequency distribution it must learn.

\section{Baseline Summary}
\label{sec:rq1_joint_view}

The baseline has two parts. First, LlamaGen gives better clean ImageNet reconstructions under the reported sample-level and distribution-level metrics. Second, the two tokenizers use their nominally equal codebooks very differently. Table~\ref{tab:rq1_joint_summary} keeps a compact version of these two facts together.

\begin{table}[t]
    \centering
    \scriptsize
    \begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrrr}
        \textbf{Tokenizer} & \textbf{PSNR} & \textbf{SSIM} & \textbf{LPIPS} & \textbf{FID} & \textbf{Active} & \textbf{Perplexity} & \textbf{Top-100} & \textbf{Codes for 99\%} \\ \hline

    \caption{Joint ImageNet summary of reconstruction quality and codebook usage. The final column reports the number of most frequent codes required to cover 99\% of token assignments.}
    \label{tab:rq1_joint_summary}

These results define the reference point for later chapters. They do not identify a single causal mechanism: the two tokenizers differ in architecture, training objective, codebook dimensionality, and normalization. What the baseline shows is more limited and more useful. On ImageNet validation, LlamaGen starts from both a stronger reconstruction baseline and a nearly full empirical vocabulary. VQGAN starts from lower reconstruction scores and a much smaller active codebook.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:rq1_discussion}

In discrete representation learning, codebook collapse---many entries going unused---is a well-documented failure mode~\cite{lancucki2020robust,yu2021vector,mentzer2023finite}. LlamaGen avoids this behavior on the 16,384-entry codebook evaluated here: every code appears on ImageNet, and the perplexity is close to the nominal vocabulary size. VQGAN shows partial collapse on the same dataset: roughly 94\% of nominal codes are inactive, and the active subset carries the reconstruction load.

The baseline is ImageNet-specific. VQGAN may activate a different code subset on other domains, so the sparse pattern observed here does not imply global codebook collapse across all possible inputs. The reconstruction advantage also cannot be attributed to any single design choice. At this stage, the result is a baseline: LlamaGen reconstructs ImageNet validation images better and uses the nominal codebook more fully; VQGAN reconstructs less accurately and uses a much smaller empirical vocabulary. Later chapters use this clean reference point when measuring robustness, locality, and distribution shift.

\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis studied discrete image tokenizers as representations in their own
right. Reconstruction quality is one part of that evaluation, but it does not
describe how a tokenizer uses its vocabulary, how stable its token assignments
are, or how local its encoder and decoder responses are. The five research
questions examine these properties for VQGAN and LlamaGen VQ-16.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}

\textbf{RQ1: reconstruction quality and empirical codebook usage.}
LlamaGen reconstructs ImageNet validation images more accurately than VQGAN on
all four reported metrics: $20.79$ versus $19.65$ PSNR, $0.558$ versus $0.489$
SSIM, $0.228$ versus $0.286$ LPIPS, and $2.19$ versus $5.06$ FID. Their
empirical vocabularies differ more sharply than their nominal configurations
suggest. LlamaGen activates all 16{,}384 codebook entries on ImageNet, whereas
VQGAN activates about 972. Nominal codebook size is therefore not an adequate
description of the representation used by a checkpoint.

\textbf{RQ2: stability under global image perturbations.}
Neither tokenizer preserves exact token identity under modest Gaussian noise.
At $\sigma=0.1$, 79.5\% of LlamaGen token positions and 75.1\% of VQGAN token
positions differ from their clean assignments. The decoded images nevertheless
retain broad visual structure, and LlamaGen remains better on reconstruction
metrics at every tested noise level. Token identity can therefore change much
more rapidly than image-level structure. Noise also concentrates usage in both
models, reducing their effective vocabularies even when raw active-code counts
change little.

\textbf{RQ3: encoder locality under local image perturbations.}
The encoder response is not confined to the perturbed patch. At low patch
noise, the far-field token flip rate is 17.4\% for VQGAN and 17.8\% for
LlamaGen; at higher noise levels, roughly one third of far-field positions
change. Masking one image patch changes 56.3\% of VQGAN token positions and
49.5\% of LlamaGen token positions outside the corresponding token region. A
local image-space intervention can therefore produce a spatially extended
change in the discrete representation.

\textbf{RQ4: decoder locality under local token edits.}
Directly editing a local token patch produces its largest pixel response near
the edited region, but measurable changes remain outside it. This decoder-side
result is preserved on ImageNet-V2. It should not be numerically ranked against
the encoder-side token-flip ratio from RQ3: the two chapters intervene in
different spaces and measure different response variables.

\textbf{RQ5: reconstruction, vocabulary usage, and spatial allocation under
distribution shift.}
Both models retain almost all codes that are active on ImageNet across the
tested shifted datasets. Distribution shift mainly reallocates probability
mass within the active vocabulary rather than activating a different one.
ImageNet-V2 and ObjectNet remain closest to the ImageNet token distribution,
while sketches, medical images, and document pages produce larger changes;
RVL-CDIP has the largest Jensen--Shannon divergence for both tokenizers.
Reconstruction quality and token-distribution shift do not follow the same
ordering. The code-specific spatial analysis suggests movement for selected
tokens, but it remains exploratory because the result depends on token choice
and frequency.

\section{Overall Conclusion}

The experiments support evaluating a discrete image tokenizer through a set of
complementary probes: reconstruction quality, empirical codebook usage, token
stability, spatial response, and distributional behavior. LlamaGen is stronger
on the measured reconstruction metrics and uses a much broader vocabulary, but
that breadth does not make its exact token identities more stable under noise.
VQGAN uses a much smaller active subset, yet its token probabilities still
change under perturbation and domain shift. A tokenizer should therefore be
treated as a component that shapes the representation and input distribution
seen by an autoregressive model, not only as a reconstruction module.

\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis compared VQGAN and LlamaGen VQ-16 to determine how two pretrained
image tokenizers with the same $16\times16$ output grid differ beyond
reconstruction quality. The study measured reconstruction, empirical codebook
usage, token stability under global noise, encoder and decoder locality, and
the response to distribution shift. Each analysis isolated the tokenizer from
the autoregressive model that would consume its tokens.

For RQ1, the reconstruction analysis established the clean baseline under
each exporter's recorded preprocessing. On ImageNet validation, LlamaGen achieved
$20.79$ rather than $19.65$ dB PSNR, $0.558$ rather than $0.489$ SSIM, $0.228$
rather than $0.286$ LPIPS, and $2.19$ rather than $5.06$ FID. The codebook
measurements revealed a larger difference between the checkpoints. LlamaGen
used all 16{,}384 nominal entries at least once, while VQGAN used about
972. The nominal vocabulary size therefore did not describe the vocabulary
used by each checkpoint.

For RQ2, global image perturbations showed that neither tokenizer
preserved exact token identity under the tested Gaussian noise. At $\sigma=0.1$
in the internal $[-1,1]$ image range (equivalent to a standard deviation of
$0.05$ in $[0,1]$ before clipping),
79.5\% of LlamaGen positions and 75.1\% of VQGAN positions differed from their
clean assignments. LlamaGen had higher PSNR to the original clean image at
every tested noise level. A separate 2{,}000-image comparison of saved clean
and noisy reconstructions found lower decoded RMSE for LlamaGen at all three
noise levels, despite its slightly higher identity-flip fraction. The
full-dataset PSNR-to-original measure and this sampled direct-change measure
answer different questions. Noise also concentrated code usage in
both models without greatly changing the raw number of active codes.

For RQ3, local image perturbations showed that the encoder response was
not confined to the perturbed patch. At low patch noise, the far-field token
flip rate was 17.4\% for VQGAN and 17.8\% for LlamaGen. Masking one image patch
changed 56.3\% of VQGAN positions and 49.5\% of LlamaGen positions outside the
corresponding token region.

For RQ4, the decoder analysis found that directly editing a
local token patch produced the largest pixel changes near the edited region,
although measurable changes remained outside it. The same pattern held on the
slightly out-of-distribution ImageNet-V2 dataset, where the largest change in
patch LPIPS relative to ImageNet validation was 0.0068. Locality is therefore
preserved approximately rather than exactly when the decoder maps token edits
back to pixels. A saved-image distance profile for the 25\% edit shows that
outside response declines with distance from the edited boundary but remains
measurable 80--96 pixels away on the sampled images.

For RQ5, both VQGAN and LlamaGen retained
almost all codes that were active on ImageNet. They did not switch to a
substantially different set of active tokens. Instead, they changed how often
they used those tokens. ImageNet-V2 remained close to the ImageNet token
distribution, while ImageNet-Sketch, BloodMNIST, and RVL-CDIP produced larger
changes. These datasets support the frequency-redistribution finding
independently of the limited ObjectNet and OrganAMNIST exports. RVL-CDIP produced the largest
Jensen--Shannon divergence for both tokenizers. The reconstruction scores do
not show a uniform ordering across these datasets, but their different image
content prevents that comparison from isolating the effect of distribution
shift on reconstruction quality.

Reconstruction, token identity, and code-frequency measurements describe
different properties of these checkpoints. High flip rates occurred in both
systems, but do not calibrate a robustness ranking across their different
codebooks. The distribution-shift results show that
an almost unchanged active set can conceal substantial frequency changes. A
tokenizer therefore determines more than how faithfully an image is rebuilt;
it also determines the sequence distribution presented to the autoregressive
model.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations_future_work}

This study compared one pretrained VQGAN checkpoint with one pretrained
LlamaGen VQ-16 checkpoint. They share a VQGAN-style encoder--quantizer--decoder
backbone, but differ in checkpoint, training recipe, codebook dimensionality,
normalization, and implementation details. The observed differences describe
the two complete systems. They do not identify which
design choice caused each result. A direct mechanistic study should modify one
tokenizer property at a time, retrain the affected models, and repeat the same
measurements. This would test the effects of codebook geometry and training
choices while controlling the remaining components.

All experiments in this thesis use $256\times256$ inputs. Their exporters used
different resize filters, as documented in the reproducibility appendix.
The local VQGAN baseline differs from the published comparison by 0.35 dB
PSNR and 0.07 FID; the cause has not been isolated
(Section~\ref{sec:rq1_reconstruction_baseline}).
Resizing and cropping
can alter datasets whose native resolution, aspect ratio, or layout differs
from ImageNet, especially document and medical images. Future comparisons
should evaluate the existing checkpoints at other supported resolutions and
test how the distribution-shift patterns depend on the chosen input size.

The ObjectNet exporters did not explicitly remove the source images' red
identification border. Its reported token frequencies and spatial contrasts
therefore describe the marked-image pipeline; in particular, the border-grid
contrast cannot be interpreted as a property of object content alone. The
local OrganAMNIST export also omitted 1{,}643 images from the official
training split. Its results describe the 57{,}187 saved images rather than the
complete release. A full-dataset replication should correct both inputs and
re-evaluate those two datasets before making border-free or full-release
claims.

The analyses of global and local perturbations used the same seeds and
intervention levels for both models, but they did not save a shared record of
the exact noise arrays and patch locations. Identical seed values do not imply
identical perturbations across separate implementations and preprocessing
pipelines; the realizations and patch locations were not matched. This
avoidable limitation was
identified after data collection. A replication should export every
intervention once and apply the same saved intervention to both tokenizers.
The global-noise sweep starts at $\sigma=0.1$ in the internal image range;
it does not characterize weaker perturbations. Clipping changes the sampled
noise distribution, particularly at the strongest level. Decoder edit modes
also differ in induced distance between codebooks, and VQGAN random edits can
select codes absent from clean ImageNet assignments. These interventions do
not identify intrinsic cross-model sensitivity or individual causal mechanisms
(Sections~\ref{subsec:rq2_perturbation_protocol} and~\ref{sec:rq4_discussion}).

The clean ImageNet PSNR comparison has a paired image-level bootstrap interval
and win rate; the other reconstruction metrics do not have analogous paired
estimates. The decoder distance-to-boundary profile uses 500 saved eight-bit
image pairs per checkpoint at one patch size; it describes spatial decay, not
a fixed-width halo or an exact receptive-field radius. The archived per-image
ImageNet grids allowed a sample-matched
positional-entropy check for ImageNet-V2, BloodMNIST, and RVL-CDIP. Per-image
grids are not available for every shifted dataset, so the remaining
comparisons could not receive the same check.
No sampling null was computed for JSD, so small divergences are descriptive
rather than significance tests. Cross-dataset reconstruction scores also
depend on image content; they do not establish preservation or loss of quality
caused by a distribution shift.

Finally, the study did not train or evaluate an autoregressive model on the
measured token streams. It therefore cannot show how token instability,
concentrated code usage, or distribution-dependent token frequencies affect
generation. Future work should train or evaluate autoregressive priors on these
streams and measure generation quality and robustness directly. Further
spatial analysis should retain per-image grids for every dataset so all
positional-entropy comparisons can use matched counts.

The code, analysis notebooks, supporting data, and instructions for reproducing
the reported results will be provided separately to the supervisor.
